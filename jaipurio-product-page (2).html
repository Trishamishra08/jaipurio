From Quarry to Doorstep

How this statue is made

Every Vinayak Art & Marble piece follows the same six-step process, unchanged for generations.

1

Block Selection

A single block of Makrana marble is chosen and checked for veining, density, and flaws before any cutting begins.

2

Rough Carving

The basic form is roughed out by hand with chisels, following traditional Ganesha iconography.

3

Detail Work

Facial features, jewellery, and drapery are carved under natural light so no tool mark is missed.

4

Hand Polishing

Fine sanding and polishing bring out the natural translucence of the marble, without chemical brighteners.

5

Quality Check

Each piece is inspected for cracks, symmetry, and finish before it's approved for sale.

6

Protective Packing

Triple-layer crating with foam corners and a wooden outer frame for pieces over 18 inches.

Why Choose This Hand-Carved Ganesha Statue

A Ganesha statue is one of the most widely gifted and displayed murtis in Indian homes — Lord Ganesha is worshipped as the remover of obstacles, invoked at the start of any new venture, from a housewarming to a wedding to a new business. This piece is carved from single-block Makrana marble by the artisans of Vinayak Art & Marble in Jaipur, using techniques passed down through generations of Rajasthani stone carvers.

Unlike mass-poured resin or composite statues, every piece here begins as raw stone. Two statues are never perfectly identical — the marble's natural veining shows through differently on each one — and the finished piece has a weight, cool touch, and permanence that resin cannot replicate.

Where to Place Your Ganesha Statue

Vastu tradition generally recommends placing a Ganesha idol facing the main entrance of the home, or in the northeast corner of a room, positioned so it faces inward rather than toward an exit. This is a general guideline, adaptable to your home's layout.

A Note on Marble Care

Marble is a natural, porous stone. It should never be cleaned with acidic products like lemon or vinegar-based cleaners, which can etch the surface over time. A soft, dry cloth for daily dusting and an occasional pH-neutral stone cleaner is all this statue needs to stay bright for decades. Learn more about the properties of marble on Wikipedia's marble page.
